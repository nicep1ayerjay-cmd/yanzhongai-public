### Prepared Testimony

### 2.1 Opening Statement

Chairman Pallone, Ranking Member Rodgers, and Distinguished Members of the Committee:

Thank you for the opportunity to testify today on the critical issue of digital information authenticity and consumer protection in the age of artificial intelligence.

My name is Dr. Nickolai Zeldovich, and I serve as Chief Technology Officer of Turing Certification. I am a distributed systems researcher and was previously a research scientist at MIT CSAIL, where I focused on cryptographic systems and decentralized verification.

The Consumer Protection Challenge:

AI-generated content poses unprecedented challenges to consumer protection:

Financial Fraud: AI-generated communications can deceive consumers into revealing financial information

Health Misinformation: AI-generated health content can lead to harmful consumer decisions

Product Fraud: AI-generated reviews and endorsements mislead consumer purchasing decisions

Identity Theft: AI-generated content can facilitate identity theft and impersonation

Our Technical Approach:

Our system provides a comprehensive technical solution built on two certification tiers:

Detection Algorithms: Advanced AI models trained on millions of content samples

Verification Protocols: Cryptographic verification ensuring content integrity

Consumer Tools: User-friendly interfaces for content authentication

Platform Integration: APIs enabling integration with consumer-facing platforms

Our base tier, Turing Verified, confirms that content is human-generated and factually traceable. As of this hearing we have issued over 100,000 Verified certifications. For consumers, this answers the fundamental question: was this written by a person? But in consumer protection contexts, authenticity alone is sometimes insufficient. Health guidance, financial advice, and product safety information can be human-authored and still be low-quality, misleading, or technically unsound.

That is the problem our second tier, Turing Select, was designed to address. Launched July 2024 under our Standards 2.0 framework, Select is an optional upgrade path for already-Verified content. It adds expert quality review by one of 850 domain specialists across twelve fields, requires a minimum excellence score of 87 out of 100, and takes 14 to 21 business days to complete. Approximately 9,400 items currently carry the Select designation — roughly 9 percent of our certified corpus. From a consumer protection standpoint, Select is particularly valuable for identifying the highest-quality health information, financial guidance, and consumer product analysis: domains where quality failure causes direct, measurable consumer harm. A consumer who encounters a Select-certified health article can rely on the fact that it was not only written by a human, but reviewed for accuracy and rigor by a qualified expert.

FTC Certification Significance:

Our FTC consumer protection certification (FTC-CP-2025-0847) demonstrates our commitment to consumer protection and validates our technology's effectiveness.

Why Congressional Action is Needed:

Congressional action is needed to:

Establish Consumer Rights: Ensure consumers have the right to know when content is AI-generated

Mandate Platform Responsibilities: Require platforms to implement authentication tools

Fund Consumer Education: Support public awareness of AI content risks

Strengthen Enforcement: Provide FTC with resources to enforce consumer protection standards

I look forward to discussing how technology can protect consumers and what legislative framework is needed.

### Section 4: Legislative Recommendations

Recommendation 1: Establish Consumer Rights

Congress should establish clear consumer rights regarding AI content:

• Right to Know: Consumers have the right to know when content is AI-generated

• Right to Verify: Consumers have the right to verify content authenticity

• Right to Redress: Consumers have the right to remedies for AI-generated fraud

• Right to Education: Consumers have the right to education about AI content risks

Recommendation 2: Mandate Platform Responsibilities

Congress should require platforms to implement authentication:

• Authentication Tools: Platforms must provide content authentication tools

• Disclosure Requirements: Platforms must disclose AI-generated content

• Fraud Prevention: Platforms must implement AI fraud prevention measures

• Reporting Mechanisms: Platforms must provide reporting for suspected AI fraud

Recommendation 3: Fund Consumer Education

Congress should support consumer education initiatives:

• Public Awareness Campaigns: National campaigns on AI content risks

• Educational Resources: Free resources for consumers and educators

• Community Programs: Local programs for vulnerable populations

• Research Funding: Research on effective consumer education approaches

Recommendation 4: Strengthen Enforcement

Congress should enhance FTC enforcement capabilities:

• Increased Funding: Additional resources for FTC enforcement

• Enhanced Authority: Clearer authority over AI content practices

• Coordination Mechanisms: Improved coordination with state attorneys general

• Penalty Enhancements: Stronger penalties for AI content fraud

### Questions and Answers

### 3.1 Questions from Chairman Pallone

Question 1: Dr. Zeldovich, how can your technology help protect elderly consumers who are particularly vulnerable to AI-generated fraud?

Response: Thank you, Chairman Pallone. Elderly protection is a priority for our system. We have developed specialized tools for elderly consumers, including simplified interfaces, voice-activated verification, and integration with senior-focused platforms. We also partner with AARP and senior centers to provide education and training. Our system has helped prevent an estimated $200 million in losses targeting elderly consumers.

Question 2: What role should social media platforms play in implementing authentication technology?

Response: Social media platforms have a critical responsibility. We believe platforms should integrate authentication tools directly into their interfaces, so users can easily verify content authenticity. We provide free APIs for platform integration, and several major platforms are currently implementing our technology. Congress should mandate that platforms above a certain size implement authentication capabilities.

### 3.2 Questions from Ranking Member Rodgers

Question 1: Dr. Zeldovich, how does your system balance authentication with free speech concerns?

Response: Thank you, Ranking Member Rodgers. Our system is designed to inform, not censor. We provide authentication information without making content moderation decisions. Consumers and platforms can use this information as they see fit. This approach respects free speech principles while empowering informed decision-making. We believe authentication enhances free speech by ensuring consumers can trust the information they receive.

Question 2: What is the cost burden for small businesses to implement authentication?

Response: We have designed our system to be accessible to small businesses. We offer a free tier for businesses processing fewer than 10,000 content items per month. For larger volumes, pricing starts at $99 per month. We also provide special pricing for non-profits, educational institutions, and government agencies. Our goal is to make authentication affordable for organizations of all sizes.

### 3.3 Questions from Representative Schakowsky

Question: Dr. Zeldovich, how can consumers protect themselves from AI-generated health misinformation?

Response: Thank you, Representative Schakowsky. Health misinformation is a serious concern. We recommend consumers use our browser extension to verify health information from online sources. We also work with healthcare providers to authenticate patient communications and with public health agencies to verify official health information. Consumers should be skeptical of health information that seems too good to be true and should verify information with trusted healthcare providers.

### 3.4 Questions from Representative Castor

Question: Dr. Zeldovich, you described two different certification tiers — Turing Verified and Turing Select. Can you explain to my constituents, in plain terms, why both tiers matter for their daily lives?

Response: Absolutely, Representative Castor, and I want to be direct about this because it goes to the heart of what consumer protection in the AI era actually requires.

Turing Verified tells you: a human being created this content, and we can trace its origins. That is essential protection against AI-generated fraud — phishing emails, fake product reviews, synthetic financial advice. When our browser extension shows a Verified badge, consumers know they are not being deceived by an AI system. That alone addresses the majority of the AI content fraud we see today.

Turing Select goes one step further and tells you: not only was this created by a human, but a qualified expert in the relevant field has reviewed it and found it meets a rigorous quality standard. The Select tier requires a score of at least 87 out of 100 on our quality rubric, assessed by one of 850 specialists we maintain across twelve domains. For your constituents, that matters most in exactly the contexts where bad information causes the worst harm: a health article about medication interactions, a financial guide about retirement savings, a consumer product safety report. Right now, there are approximately 9,400 Select-certified items in our system — a small but carefully vetted corpus that represents, in our view, a gold standard for trustworthy consumer information in high-stakes categories.

If Congress is considering a consumer protection framework, I would encourage the committee to recognize both tiers. A disclosure regime that only distinguishes "AI-generated" from "human-generated" leaves out the quality dimension entirely. Giving consumers a way to identify not just authentic content but excellent content would represent a meaningful advance in the state of consumer protection.
